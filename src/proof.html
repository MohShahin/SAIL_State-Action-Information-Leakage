Abstract

Offline reinforcement learning for sepsis treatment learns a policy from a state representation built substantially from the SOFA organ-dysfunction score. We show this construction admits two provable failure modes — a scoring-rule non-identifiability and a window-overlap mechanism whose behavior, contrary to naive intuition, is not monotonic in window length, for reasons we derive from first principles. We place both in a causal graph and show the resulting state is formally a descendant of the prior treatment action — explaining why an empirical predictability gap is necessary but not sufficient evidence of leakage specifically. We close with a falsifiable, pre-registered decision procedure for the one claim that requires an experiment rather than a proof.

Setup and notation

Decision times $t = 1,\dots,T_i$ per ICU stay, in 4-hour bins. $A_t$: vasopressor active following decision point $t$. $S_t$: the state vector at $t$, built from lookback window $[t-w,t)$. $U_t$: the patient's true, unobserved physiology. A well-posed state should not be a near-deterministic function of $A_{t-1}$ in a way that lets a model recover $A_t$ through $A_{t-1}$'s influence on $S_t$ alone, independent of what $S_t$ says about $U_t$.

Theorem 1 — non-identifiability of the SOFA cardiovascular score

The cardiovascular SOFA subscore (Vincent et al., 1996) is a function of mean arterial pressure and active drug/dose $D$:

$$\sigma_{cardio}(\text{MAP}, D) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{MAP} \geq 70,\ D = \emptyset \\ 1 & \text{MAP} < 70,\ D = \emptyset \\ 2 & D \in \{\text{dopamine} \leq 5,\ \text{dobutamine (any)}\} \\ 3 & D \in \{\text{dopamine} > 5,\ \text{epi} \leq 0.1,\ \text{norepi} \leq 0.1\} \\ 4 & D \in \{\text{dopamine} > 5,\ \text{epi} > 0.1,\ \text{norepi} > 0.1\} \end{cases}$$

Statement
On the domain where $D$ crosses a dose threshold, $\sigma_{cardio}$ is constant in MAP: $\partial \sigma_{cardio} / \partial \text{MAP} = 0$ there.
Proof
Inspect the three non-zero branches directly — none contains MAP as an argument. Fix $D=$ dopamine at 6 mcg/kg/min. For any $\text{MAP} \in \{40, 55, 70, 90,\dots\}$, $\sigma_{cardio}(\text{MAP},D)=4$ identically. Two physiologically opposite states (severe hypotension vs. normotension) map to an identical score. Exhaustive over the branch's domain — settled by the definition, not by sampling.
∎

Corollary 1.1. For any timestep where $D\ne\emptyset$ and dose exceeds threshold, $S_t$'s cardiovascular feature is a deterministic function of $A_{t-1}$, independent of $U_t$. Empirically, this domain condition holds for 32.3% (4h window) / 28.6% (24h window) of eligible timesteps overall, and 100% of already-treated timesteps — see the live calculator on the walkthrough page to verify this yourself.

Theorem 2 — reconstructibility under naive feature removal

$\text{sofa\_total} = \sum_{i=1}^{6}\text{subscore}_i$, a deterministic linear sum including $\sigma_{cardio}$.

Statement
Removing $\sigma_{cardio}$ while retaining $\text{sofa\_total}$ (state variant C) does not remove the information $\sigma_{cardio}$ carries.
Proof
$\sigma_{cardio} = \text{sofa\_total} - \sum_{i\ne cardio}\text{subscore}_i$. Every term on the right is observable in variant C. The reconstruction is an algebraic identity, exact rather than approximate.
∎

Corollary 2.1. A removal scheme defeats Theorem 2 only if it recomputes every quantity $\sigma_{cardio}$ is reconstructible from — $\text{sofa\_total}$ itself must be recomputed from a treatment-independent proxy. Confirmed empirically: variant C's AUROC (0.885) sits close to the full state's (0.900); variant D — which recomputes the total per Corollary 2.1 — drops to 0.792, close to the physiology-only floor (0.791).

Window-overlap leakage: what is provable, and a correction

For decision point $t$, window $w$, let $O_t(w)$ be total treatment-hours overlapping $[t-w,t)$. Contamination fraction: $$c_t(w) = \min\left(1, \frac{O_t(w)}{w}\right)$$

Proposition 1 — monotonicity of absolute overlap
For $w_1 < w_2$: $O_t(w_1) \le O_t(w_2)$.
Proof
$[t-w_1,t) \subset [t-w_2,t)$ — nested windows. $O_t(w)$ measures treatment time intersecting a set that only grows as $w$ increases.
∎

Why the fraction is not similarly monotonic — the important correction. $c_t(w)=O_t(w)/w$ is a ratio of a monotone non-decreasing numerator to a strictly increasing denominator. Differentiating: $$\frac{dc_t}{dw} \propto \frac{dO_t}{dw} - c_t(w)$$ so $c_t(w)$ increases at $w$ precisely when the marginal treatment density in the newly-included slice exceeds the average density accumulated so far — the same relationship as a marginal-cost curve crossing an average-cost curve. This is provable, not an artifact, and it correctly predicts what this project actually measured: the 4-hour window showed a higher >50%-contamination rate (34.3%) than the 24-hour window (28.7–30.8% across runs) — consistent with, not contradictory to, this framework.

The causal graph, and why H3 needs an experiment

Proposition 2. There exists a directed path $A_{t-1}\to\sigma_{cardio,t}\to S_t$. Proof. Direct from the edges above. ∎ This means $S_t$ is a descendant of $A_{t-1}$ — not a novel finding in the abstract (using a variable affected by treatment as a nominal covariate is documented in Hernán & Robins, Causal Inference: What If), but here it follows from a specific, provable graph edge (Theorem 1).

A classifier predicting $A_t$ from $S_t$ (Experiment 2) cannot, from AUROC alone, attribute its performance between the coral solid path (definitional leak) and the coral dashed path (ordinary persistence, unrelated to SOFA). This is why H3 requires an experiment, not a proof.

The open conjecture, stated as a falsifiable test

Let $\text{AUROC}(k)$ denote Experiment 5(b)'s offset-decay AUROC at offset $k \in \{0,1,2,4,8\}$ bins. Define $\Delta = \text{AUROC}(0) - \text{AUROC}(8)$.

Prediction A
$\Delta \ge 0.10$, CIs don't overlap → supports genuine, temporally-specific leak
Prediction B
$\Delta < 0.10$, or CIs overlap → treatment persistence explains most of the gap
Falsification
flat curve + variant E already matching A → mechanisms real but practically negligible here
