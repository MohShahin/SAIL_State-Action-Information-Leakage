SAIL: State–Action Information Leakage

A reinforcement-learning model can appear to learn a sound sepsis treatment policy when the state it's given already contains information about the treatment it's about to recommend. This project measures — and in two cases, proves outright — how often that happens.

Is the model learning the patient — or learning the treatment?

Both pipelines below look identical from the outside — same architecture, same training procedure. The difference is invisible unless you look at what the state actually contains.

INTENDED PIPELINE

The model learns a genuine mapping from a patient's condition to a clinical decision.

WHAT SAIL FINDS INSTEAD

The model partly recovers the answer from the question — SOFA's own construction and window timing both provably do this.

Why this matters

The concern isn't prediction accuracy on its own — it's whether apparent performance reflects genuine patient information, or something else entirely.

MECHANISM 1

State → action confounding

The SOFA cardiovascular subscore is scored directly from vasopressor dose, not from blood pressure alone. Above a dose threshold, the score stops responding to physiology entirely — this is a proven fact about the scoring function's definition, not a measured tendency.

MECHANISM 2

Treatment overlap

A "current state" snapshot is built by aggregating over a lookback window. When that window overlaps a period when treatment was already running, the snapshot carries treatment history into it that it was supposed to precede.

Formalize it

Simple
Technical

Think of the cardiovascular part of the SOFA score as a rule with two inputs: blood pressure, and whatever medication is currently running. Once the medication dose crosses a certain level, the rule stops looking at blood pressure at all — the score is entirely determined by the medication. That means the "current condition" the model sees can, in that regime, be nothing more than a readout of what was already prescribed.

The cardiovascular SOFA subscore $\sigma_{cardio}(\text{MAP}, D)$ is piecewise in mean arterial pressure and active drug/dose $D$. On the domain where $D$ crosses a dose threshold, none of the corresponding branches reference MAP:

$$\frac{\partial \sigma_{cardio}}{\partial \text{MAP}} = 0 \quad \text{whenever } D \text{ exceeds threshold}$$

This is a proven non-identifiability result (Theorem 1), not a statistical tendency — verifiable by inspecting the scoring rule's branches directly. Full statement, proof, and the algebraic reconstruction argument for why naive feature removal doesn't fix it (Theorem 2) are on the formal proof page.

-- adult, first-ICU-stay, Sepsis-3-qualifying, vasopressor-exposed stays
WITH ranked_icu AS (
  SELECT ie.subject_id, ie.hadm_id, ie.stay_id,
    ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY ie.subject_id ORDER BY ie.intime) AS rn
  FROM `physionet-data.mimiciv_3_1_icu.icustays` ie
  JOIN `physionet-data.mimiciv_3_1_hosp.patients` p USING(subject_id)
  WHERE p.anchor_age >= 18
)
SELECT f.* FROM ranked_icu f WHERE f.rn = 1;
-- verified: 11,354 stays, exact match across five independent runs
