The two mechanisms

Both are provable — see the formal proof page for the full theorems. This page is the intuitive deep-dive: what each mechanism is, why it happens, and how much it actually measures out to.

Mechanism 1 — State → action confounding

The cardiovascular SOFA subscore is defined as a function of two things: mean arterial pressure, and whichever vasopressor drug and dose is currently running. Above a dose threshold, the scoring rule's branches stop referencing blood pressure entirely — two patients with opposite blood pressure, same dose, get the identical score.

Removing the subscore alone isn't sufficient: the overall SOFA total is a deterministic sum of all six organ subscores, so the cardiovascular contribution is exactly reconstructible from the total minus the other five — this is why variant C (subscore removed, total kept) barely drops in predictability (0.885 vs. 0.900), while variant D (total recomputed from a treatment-independent proxy) drops close to the physiology floor (0.792 vs. 0.791).

$$\sigma_{cardio} = \text{sofa\_total} - \sum_{i \ne cardio}\text{subscore}_i$$

Full statement and proof: Theorem 1 and Theorem 2.

Mechanism 2 — Overlapping temporal windows

A "current state" snapshot is built by aggregating physiology over a lookback window. If that window overlaps a period when treatment was already running, the snapshot is contaminated with treatment history — even with no drug-dose term in any scoring formula at all.

The counterintuitive part: a naive "wider window, more contamination" intuition is false in general. Absolute overlap hours are provably non-decreasing in window length (windows nest), but the fraction is a ratio of a growing numerator to a growing denominator — it rises exactly when marginal treatment density exceeds average density accumulated so far, the same relationship as a marginal-cost curve crossing an average-cost curve. This is why a 4-hour window measured higher >50%-contamination than the 24-hour window in this project's own data.

Full statement and proof: Section 5, window-overlap leakage.
